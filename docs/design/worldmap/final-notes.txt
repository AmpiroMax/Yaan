THE HARD CONSTRAINT FIRST: THE COASTLINE DID NOT MOVE
==============================================================================

The carve is written so that it CANNOT move a coast, and then it is measured anyway.

  BY CONSTRUCTION.  h_new = min(h, max(target, 21)).  It runs only inside the land branch of
  the heightmap builder, it can only LOWER a cell, and it can never take one below 21 while
  FMG's land threshold is 20. So the set {h >= 20} is untouched whatever the route does.

  BY MEASUREMENT.  iteration 5's .map and iteration 6's .map are saves of the SAME 100x100
  grid graph, so grid.cells.h can be compared cell for cell — and if no cell crosses 20, the
  pack graph FMG builds from that grid is identical too, and with it every coastline, island
  and lake shore. That is stronger than comparing two pictures:

  and the pack graph came back the same size: 6036 cells, 4319 of them land — iteration 5's own

  WHY THIS ROUTE AND NOT A STRAIGHT ONE. Crownhaven (u .663 v .443) and Saltcliff
  (u .660 v .565) stand almost on the same meridian, 0.12 map widths apart, with the Yaan
  divide between them: a river joining that pair directly is 400 km long and drains nothing.
  The water is in the WEST — the interior is arid (prec 0-5 over the whole Venedian centre
  against 19-31 in the western marches), so a river's size here is decided by WHICH cells it
  drains and not by how many. And Saltcliff cannot be reached from the west along the coast:
  between u .61 and u .65 the shore of the Midday Sea rises to v .555 and a bay bites in at
  u .625, so any axis there stands one or two cells from open water and the river simply
  leaves. Measured three times, from three different lines. Hence: gather the water in the
  west, carry it east down the northern plain, and come at Saltcliff from due north.

  WHICH OF THE TWELVE STAND ON IT (distance from the city's cell to the Volkhona system,
  in cells; a cell is 56 km here):

  STONEFORD — THE ONE THING THAT WAS ASKED FOR AND DID NOT COME OFF. Its passport is 'the
  bend of a big navigable river' and the owner asked for the Volkhona to be taken through it
  if it could be. The Venedian arm IS carved through Stoneford and Stoneford does stand on a

THE TSAR'S PORTAGE / ГОСУДАРЕВ ВОЛОК
==============================================================================

  An ADDED LABEL and a MARKER at u=0.592 v=0.459 — FMG has no entity for a place that is not a
  burg, so a named landform is an added label exactly as the four seas are.

  Where 'between the two rivers' is is not a matter of taste, it is the WATERSHED, and a
  watershed is measurable — but WHICH TWO POINTS to measure between is a decision, and the
  obvious one is wrong. FMG draws a river only from the cell where flux first reaches 30, and
  the Yaren drains five arid cells, so its drawn source is three cells from its own mouth, out
  on the rim of the Crownhaven fjord; a straight line from there to the Volkhona crosses the
  gulf's shoulder at h 48, and the first run duly reported a 322 km portage over seven hundred
  metres of mountain. So the search is over the two CARVED VALLEYS instead: every pair of
  points on the Yaren's channel and the Volkhona's trunk within 0.09 map widths of each other,
  the line walked cell by cell, and the pair whose HIGHEST cell is lowest wins. It runs from
  u=0.596 v=0.461 to u=0.555 v=0.446. The three numbers that are the whole claim:

    Yaren's bed        h 28
    the saddle         h 30
    Volkhona's bed     h 24
    the carry          247 km (4.4 cells) — the profile, cell by cell:
      28 28 30 30 30 30 30 30 29 29 29 29 29 29 29 25 25 25 24 24 24

  Two height units above the Yaren's bed and six above the Volkhona's — call it fifty to a
  hundred metres over 247 km of flat watershed, which is the Volga-Don situation and is what a
  state-run carry is for.

  IT IS NOT CUT, AND THAT IS THE POINT. A portage a boat can float through is not a portage.
  The two channels are carved to within 0.04 map widths of each other and the ground between
  them is left exactly as iteration 4 built it — the saddle is iteration 4's own plain.
  ONE CELL IS 56 km HERE, so the shortest carry this atlas can draw at all is about 170 km;
  this one is 247.

HOW THE CARVE WORKS, AND THE TWO THINGS IT COST TO LEARN
==============================================================================

A channel is a polyline in screen units carrying an explicit THALWEG PROFILE: one target
height per vertex, interpolated by arclength, with a flat floor out to 0.32 of the half-width
and a wall that climbs back to the ambient terrain by the lip. Three properties are
structural: it can only lower land, never below 21; the profile is monotonic and the builder
re-checks it; and the valley has WALLS, which is the only reason routing a river is possible
at all — D8 drainage sees neighbours, not distant sea, so a river will follow a 3-unit trench
past a coast that is five cells away and ten units lower.

1. THE BANK NOISE WAS ON THE FLOOR. The roughness that keeps the trench from reading as a
   ruled line was written with weight (1-w), which is 1 ON THE THALWEG. Heights are stored as
   integers and the floor only falls 0.2 per vertex, so +-1.1 of noise turned a monotonic
   thalweg into a chain of puddles and the Venedian arm's water went straight back out of the
   old mouth. It is 4*w*(1-w) now — zero on the floor, zero at the lip, greatest half way up
   the bank. The drainage trace in preview6.mjs is what caught it, not the eye.

2. AN INTEGER HEIGHTMAP HAS NO GENTLE GRADIENTS. The whole system has six height units to
   spend between the Venedian plain and the sea, over 0.45 map widths. Spread evenly that
   rounds to flat runs ten cells long, and on a flat run FMG's own tiebreak (alterHeights
   adds distance-from-coast/100) sends the water to the NEAREST coast, which is the one the
   river was being routed away from. The floors are now chosen so that the ROUNDED sequence
   steps down, and the steps are placed where they are needed rather than evenly.
