He is describing crown plasticity, and he split it by species before we did.

PLASTICITY, MEASURED END TO END AND NOT ASSERTED

  Instrument: a 7x7 lattice of ONE species at spacing s, through the real
  analyse_neighbourhood, into build_flora_mesh; the centre tree is the subject
  because a tree inside a stand has neighbours on every bearing. Built crown
  diameter of that tree, cards only.

      species          6 m     10 m     15 m     open (40 m)   at 6 m
      oak (broadleaf)  6.4 m    9.0 m   12.9 m      16.4 m       39 %
      pine (conifer)   8.5 m    8.8 m    8.9 m       8.9 m       96 %

  That is his sentence as a table: the conifer holds one radius, the broadleaf
  does not. CROWN_PLASTICITY_BROADLEAF 0.45 / _CONIFER 0.05, derivations in
  NUMBERS.

  THE BIRCH SHOWS NOTHING, AND THAT IS CORRECT RATHER THAN BROKEN. Its crown is
  6-10 m across (which is the real silver birch: Woodland Trust / Van den Berk
  give 15-25 m tall and 6-10 m of spread), and at a 10 m lattice two birches do
  not reach each other at all — so nothing crowds them and nothing should. The
  species that demonstrates shyness at our spacing is the OAK, whose open crown
  is 16.4 m. Tuning the birch until it demonstrated the effect would have been
  fitting the tree to the demo.

  WHAT IT SHOWS: bare dark limbs with clumps of leaf at their ENDS. You can
  follow every branch from the bole to its tip through open air, and the leaf
  sits on the last stretch of it like a bud. A broadleaf crown is a MASS — from
  underneath you should not be able to trace the branch system at all.

  Measured on the frame, per material, pitched UP into the crowns:
      background 47.6 %   WOOD in front 28.6 %   FOLIAGE in front 20.5 %
      wood : foliage = 1.4 : 1

  In a view aimed into a broadleaf canopy at eye level, wood outweighing leaf
  is the whole complaint in one number.

WHY IT IS THIS WAY, AND IT IS TODAY'S OWN DOING

  "Leaves grow out of branches" (the user, this morning) was implemented as
  foliage masses hung on SHOOT ENDS — gather_shoot_anchors, and the leaf budget
  capped by the tip count. That fixed the leaf-to-wood gap it was written for
  (oak 1.39 -> 0.37 m) and it put every leaf at the end of a twig. A real tree
  carries leaves along the OUTER THIRD of every branch, not at its tip; the
  interior of the crown is what fills the mass.

  This is a diagnosis with a mechanism and it has NOT been tried, so it is
  written as the next thing to test and not as the answer.
