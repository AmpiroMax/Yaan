      pine (conifer)   8.5 m    8.8 m    8.9 m       8.9 m       96 %

  That is his sentence as a table: the conifer holds one radius, the broadleaf
  does not. CROWN_PLASTICITY_BROADLEAF 0.45 / _CONIFER 0.05, derivations in
  NUMBERS.

  THE BIRCH SHOWS NOTHING, AND THAT IS CORRECT RATHER THAN BROKEN. Its crown is
  6-10 m across (which is the real silver birch: Woodland Trust / Van den Berk
  give 15-25 m tall and 6-10 m of spread), and at a 10 m lattice two birches do
  not reach each other at all — so nothing crowds them and nothing should. The
  species that demonstrates shyness at our spacing is the OAK, whose open crown
  is 16.4 m. Tuning the birch until it demonstrated the effect would have been
  fitting the tree to the demo.

  WHAT IT SHOWS: bare dark limbs with clumps of leaf at their ENDS. You can
  follow every branch from the bole to its tip through open air, and the leaf
  sits on the last stretch of it like a bud. A broadleaf crown is a MASS — from
  underneath you should not be able to trace the branch system at all.

  Measured on the frame, per material, pitched UP into the crowns:
      background 47.6 %   WOOD in front 28.6 %   FOLIAGE in front 20.5 %
      wood : foliage = 1.4 : 1

  In a view aimed into a broadleaf canopy at eye level, wood outweighing leaf
  is the whole complaint in one number.

WHY IT IS THIS WAY, AND IT IS TODAY'S OWN DOING

  "Leaves grow out of branches" (the user, this morning) was implemented as
  foliage masses hung on SHOOT ENDS — gather_shoot_anchors, and the leaf budget
  capped by the tip count. That fixed the leaf-to-wood gap it was written for
  (oak 1.39 -> 0.37 m) and it put every leaf at the end of a twig. A real tree
  carries leaves along the OUTER THIRD of every branch, not at its tip; the
  interior of the crown is what fills the mass.

  This is a diagnosis with a mechanism and it has NOT been tried, so it is
  written as the next thing to test and not as the answer.

THE BRANCH-SPAN LEVER — MEASURED ACROSS ITS RANGE AND REFUTED (13.08.2026)
================================================================================

THE TARGET, NAMED BEFORE THE CHANGE (LEAD's, and it is the frame's own number):
in the view pitched UP into the canopy, FOLIAGE in front must overtake WOOD in
front — today 28.8 % wood against 18.6 % leaf, so the sign has to flip. Two
things were fenced as untouchable: the leaf-to-wood gap must not open (oak
0.37 m) and the built crown must not shrink.

THE LEVER: FOLIAGE_BRANCH_SPAN, the outer fraction of every branch that may
carry leaf, measured ALONG THE BRANCH — distance from the site to its own
branch tip, over that branch's whole length. It replaces a PROXY: the rule was
enforced on a node's RADIUS, which stands in for "how far out am I" only as well
as the branching pattern allows.

THE FIRST SWEEP CAME OUT FLAT and that was an error in my own instrument worth
recording: 1.0 -> 0.20 moved nothing, because the radius gate had already
excluded every inboard shoot BEFORE the span gate saw it. Two gates on one
quantity, and the one under test sat downstream of the one that decides. A
second hook (DFN_FLORA_OUTERR) was added to find which gate binds; neither did.

MEASURED ON THE FRAME, which is where the target lives, material door, same pose:

    FOLIAGE_BRANCH_SPAN 1.00 (no restriction)   wood 28.8 %  leaf 18.6 %  0.64
    FOLIAGE_BRANCH_SPAN 0.30 (the outer third)  wood 30.1 %  leaf 15.4 %  0.51

IT MOVES THE TARGET THE WRONG WAY. Restricting leaf to the outer third of each
branch thins the crown, because the mass BUDGET is fixed: the same masses are
packed into fewer eligible places and the separation rule drops some of them.
The leaf was already at the outer end; there was nothing to move outward.

So the lever is left at 1.00, which is no restriction, and the gate stays only
because it measures the right quantity where the old code measured a proxy.

WHAT DOES MOVE IT, COSTED RATHER THAN ARGUED (DFN_FLORA_MASSES, a verification
hook — the leaf budget was fenced off from this change on purpose, so it was
measured, not shipped):

    masses     leaf : wood     oak triangles (wood + cards)   budget 1300
    x1.0          0.64          802 + 198 = 1000
    x2.0          0.83          802 + 368 = 1170
    x3.5          1.06  <- flips   802 + 827 = 1629            OVER
    x3.5, wood cut to 40 %
                  0.94          356 + 246 =  602               well under

The sign flips at about x3.5 the leaf budget and nowhere else. It costs 1629
triangles against a 1300 ceiling, so it is a DESIGN decision and not a flora
one. The last row is the interesting one and it was not predicted: cutting the
wood to 40 % at FULL detail as well takes the tree to 602 triangles — three
fifths of today's cost — and still reaches 0.94, because cutting wood also cuts
the leaf budget through the tip cap (carried = min(clusters, tips*4)).
