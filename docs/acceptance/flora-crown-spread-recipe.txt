HOW TO REPRODUCE

  # BEFORE — the zero-dose control arm (flora as of 11.08.2026)
  DFN_FLORA_CONTROL=1 \
  DFN_RESTORE=docs/acceptance/flora-canopy-speckle-pose.txt \
  DFN_WIND_FREEZE=120 DFN_NULL_PHYSICS=1 \
  DFN_INTERNAL_RES=640x360 DFN_PALETTE=0 \
  DFN_CAPTURE_DIR=<dir> DFN_CAPTURE_AFTER=4 ./build_flora/engine/app/dfn_app

  # AFTER — the shipped arm. Same command, without DFN_FLORA_CONTROL.

  # THE GREAT OAK — same command plus DFN_FLORA_GREAT_OAK=3 (one oak in
  # sixteen is promoted to a great oak and carries the golden chain).
  # DFN_FLORA_GREAT_OAK=2 promotes every oak, for a close-up of the structure.

  DFN_NULL_PHYSICS=1 IS NOT COSMETIC: without it the restored player falls out
  of the world during the four-second settle and the capture is taken from
  y = -71 m, looking up at the underside of the terrain. It looks like a
  vertically flipped screenshot and it cost a run.

WHAT THE CONTROL ARM PUTS BACK (and nothing else)
  - maturity from core's flat scatter scale (0.8-1.2) instead of the
    25/60/12/3 tier draw;
  - no crown allometry, no per-instance width draw;
  - the oak's crown width 0.70 -> 0.48 and its crown base 0.35 -> 0.40, the
    birch's width back to 0.34 (the two species rows this change moved);
  - the mid-crown-widest envelope profile;
  - the lean back to crowding-only, capped at 0.12 rad.

WHAT TO LOOK FOR

  BEFORE: near-identical trees. Every trunk the same height, every crown the
  same small tuft, every stem vertical. This is the "plantation" design §5.8
  ruled against — and the cure had been written down as sixteen NUMBERS rows
  since 09.08 without being connected to anything.

  AFTER: three changes, all visible in one frame.
   1. HEIGHT SPREAD — giants, mature trees and a sub-mature layer under them.
   2. WIDTH — crowns are wide masses rather than tufts, and they differ in
      width independently of height (two trees of the same height do not have
      the same crown).
   3. LEAN — the trunks are off vertical and they agree about the direction.
      That agreement is the point (LANDSCAPE §10.3.1); a frame where each tree
      leaned its own way would be a failure wearing the same numbers.

  GREAT OAK: the branch system reads as STRUCTURE — recursive ramification with
  sky between the major limbs. Per docs/GIANT_OAKS.md §4 that gap is the whole
  test: an envelope-filled crown cannot produce one, because its silhouette is
  a single-valued function of angle. Positive gap => structure exists.

WHAT IS NOT IN THESE FRAMES, AND IS OPEN
  - The great oak at its own read distance. It is readable as an object to
    2.4 km (§1 of GIANT_OAKS) and every frame here is taken from under it, so
    "очень большие как горы" is NOT yet demonstrated. Needs a distant vantage.
  - Real placement. DFN_FLORA_GREAT_OAK is a capture-only stand-in; rarity, the
    sea cliff and the named tree are core's and design's.
  - The great oak's crown is thinner than intended: the foliage count is capped
    by the fractal grower's TIP COUNT, not by cluster_count, so a fuller crown
    means more depth or a bigger node budget, not more clusters.

  # 400 m back, 70 m up, looking into the oak mass. One oak in sixteen promoted.
  DFN_FLORA_GREAT_OAK=1 DFN_RESTORE=<pose> DFN_WIND_FREEZE=120 \
  DFN_NULL_PHYSICS=1 DFN_INTERNAL_RES=640x360 DFN_PALETTE=0 \
  DFN_CAPTURE_DIR=<dir> DFN_CAPTURE_AFTER=6 ./build_flora/engine/app/dfn_app
  # pose = the speckle pose with pos_y = 70, pos_z = 1180, pitch = -0.08.

WHY THIS FRAME WAS TAKEN BEFORE FIXING ANYTHING: at a kilometre a sparse crown
either reads as a landmark silhouette or reads as nothing, and those are
different next steps. Shooting first was cheaper than repairing first, and it
was right — the frame did not say "too sparse", it said something else entirely.

WHAT IT FOUND (two defects, both invisible from under the tree AND to a green
49/49 suite; both then measured in the generator rather than guessed at):

  1. THE GREAT OAK EMITTED ZERO CARDS. Every variant, no foliage at all. The
     card legibility floor exists at TWO sites one screen apart in
     FloraBuild.cpp; the first was re-derived for large crowns and the second
     still read `0.22 * crown_r`, which on a 40 m crown radius is an 8.8 m
     minimum half-width against the 8.4 m the species actually produces. The
     tree that had been photographed twice as "structure with thin foliage" had
     no foliage whatsoever, and both frames were read as a density problem.
  2. THE WOOD REACHED TWICE THE DECLARED HEIGHT. Recursive ramification was
     unclipped, so a 46 m great oak grew to 90.8 m — the species height band is
     a cross-zone contract (core's canopy occlusion, design's C4). That is what
     made the giants tower in the BEFORE frame: not size, overshoot.

VERDICT ON "ОЧЕНЬ БОЛЬШИЕ КАК ГОРЫ" — NOT YET, AND THE FRAME SAYS WHY.
With both fixed, the giants stop towering (correct: they are now 40-45 m against
a 25-30 m canopy) and at 400 m they MERGE INTO THE CANOPY instead of reading as
landmarks. Their 60 m crown is wider than anything around them and it still does
not read, because they stand INSIDE a 12-18 m lattice of ordinary oaks whose
crowns now touch. This is docs/GIANT_OAKS.md §2 arriving as a measurement: the
rarity is not a density setting, it is the condition under which the object can
be seen at all. A giant needs its own clearing, and that is core's and design's
to place — flora cannot buy it with geometry.

flora-great-oak-under-669f1a7b.png is the near view with the crown repaired: a
limb crossing the whole frame overhead and a canopy mass above it. That one is
the "one convincing mountain" from underneath.
