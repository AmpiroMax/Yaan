WHAT THE USER SAID (13.08.2026, verbatim)

  крона от ствола отходит, значит листья и ствол живут разной жизнью — плохо,
  листья должны из веток расти
  деревья — не объекты физики, значит их просто малюют, а не строят 3д модели

He arrived at the diagnosis before we did and stated it more precisely: what was
written was a FUNCTION THAT DRAWS A THING, not a thing that is built. The crown
was assembled by filling an ENVELOPE — branches grown toward a cloud of
attraction points, and the foliage then placed at the CENTROID of a merged
cluster of those points. A centroid is not on a branch; it cannot be, it is a
mean. So there was no relation between leaf and wood BY CONSTRUCTION, and the
crown came away from the trunk.

It is the same argument that explained two earlier defects, and docs/GIANT_OAKS.md
§4 records it: the dome cloud's outline was a single-valued function of one
azimuth, so a gap in it was impossible in principle; the great oak's envelope
could not produce sky between its major limbs for the same reason.

HOW TO REPRODUCE

  # BEFORE — the zero-dose arm. Crowns built the old way: space colonization
  # into the envelope, foliage on merged cloud centroids.
  DFN_FLORA_CROWN=1 \
  DFN_TOUR=1 DFN_TOUR_DIR=<dir> DFN_FLORA_PROBE=1 \
  DFN_WIND_FREEZE=120 DFN_NULL_PHYSICS=1 \
  DFN_INTERNAL_RES=640x360 DFN_PALETTE=0 DFN_TIME=0.5 DFN_MENU=0 \
  ./build_flora/engine/app/dfn_app

  # AFTER — the shipped arm. The same command WITHOUT DFN_FLORA_CROWN.
  # DFN_FLORA_PROBE=2 is the treeline pair.
  # Add DFN_FLORA_GREAT_OAK=2 for the great-oak pair (every oak promoted).
  # Then: python3 tools/archive_frame.py <src> <dst> 640

  DFN_NULL_PHYSICS=1 IS NOT COSMETIC (inherited from the crown-spread recipe):
  without it the restored player falls out of the world during the settle.
  AND PIPE THROUGH `tee`, NOT `>`: a plain redirect makes dfn_app exit
  immediately on this machine (found by `light`, 13.08, at the cost of a run).

WHAT DFN_FLORA_CROWN=1 PUTS BACK, AND NOTHING ELSE
  - the card species grow their crowns by space colonization again, and hang
    their foliage on merged cloud centroids (gather_foliage_anchors);
  - the great oak keeps its fractal grower — it never had a colonizing fallback
    — but goes back to one foliage mass per grower TIP;
  - the first-order limbs leave the bole from its last node again;
  - the growth bound goes back to a cylinder at the full crown radius.
  Species widths, heights, leans, card sizes, tone bands, budgets, the atlas and
  every other zone are IDENTICAL between the arms. It is a second door on
  purpose: DFN_FLORA_CONTROL restores the whole 11.08 stage at once and would
  answer a different question (Rule 48 — a control binds only the measurements
  in which it itself moves).

WHAT TO LOOK FOR

  NEAR CANOPY (flora-leaves-from-branches-near-*). The frame is pitched up into
  the crowns, which is where the complaint lives.
   BEFORE: the two foreground trees carry a compact dark blob ABOVE a bare pole,
     with air between the top of the trunk and the leaf mass. That gap is the
     user's sentence.
   AFTER: limbs fan out of the bole and the leaf masses sit ALONG them; you can
     follow wood into the foliage, and there is sky through the crown between
     the limbs rather than only around its outline.

  TREELINE (flora-leaves-from-branches-treeline-*). The same change at 70-200 m.
   The crowns read as branched rather than as blobs — and honestly, the wood is
   more prominent here than before. Recorded rather than framed away.

  GREAT OAK (flora-great-oak-structure-*, DFN_FLORA_GREAT_OAK=2). The branch
   system reads as STRUCTURE: a limb crossing the whole frame overhead, sky
   between the majors. This is the §4 test, and on this species it is decisive
   (see the ambiguity number below: 0.078 -> 0.318).

WHAT WAS MEASURED, AND WITH WHAT

  Instrument: an offline harness over build_flora_mesh (8 variants/species),
  because the quantities are properties of the GEOMETRY and a frame cannot carry
  them. Three states are reported: git HEAD before the change, the zero-dose arm
  of the shipped binary, and the shipped arm. HEAD and the control differ
  because the species TABLE (cluster sizes and counts) is not behind the door —
  the door isolates the CONSTRUCTION, which is what the user's sentence is about.

  (a) LEAF-TO-WOOD GAP — distance from a leaf card's own SURFACE to the nearest
      wood. Measured on the surface, not the centroid: the question is whether
      there is air between leaf and branch, and a centroid makes the number a
      function of card SIZE (Rule 41).
                     HEAD          control       shipped
        DaleOak      1.39 / 4.59   1.21 / 3.89   0.60 / 2.56   (mean / worst, m)
        RiverBirch   0.62 / 1.51   0.51 / 1.73   0.21 / 1.13
        ValeWillow   0.83 / 2.83   0.85 / 2.75   0.38 / 1.55
        GreatOak     3.89 / 13.04  2.35 / 16.76  0.91 / 4.25
        HighlandPine 0.24 / 1.45   0.24 / 1.39   0.24 / 1.39

  (b) SILHOUETTE AMBIGUITY — share of raster columns of the projected FOLIAGE
      where sky is visible both above and below leaf, 8 bearings, floored at the
      crown base. GIANT_OAKS §4's test.
        DaleOak 0.189 -> 0.386   RiverBirch 0.663 -> 0.705
        ValeWillow 0.325 -> 0.470   GreatOak 0.078 -> 0.318
      READ IT WITH (c) OR NOT AT ALL: a crown raises this number by simply
      having less foliage, and that is confetti, not structure.

  (c) FLORA_CROWN_OPTICAL_DEPTH (design §10.15.2) — presented card area over the
      crown's own presented silhouette area, worst of 36 azimuths. "Layers of
      leaf", and it cannot be bought with width.
        DaleOak 3.81 -> 6.27   RiverBirch 1.67 -> 2.95
        ValeWillow 3.36 -> 6.33   GreatOak 81.21 -> 19.96
      THE GIANT'S 81 IS THE FINDING. GIANT_OAKS §4 predicted in words that a
      great oak without gaps would read as a green hill; 81 layers of leaf at an
      ambiguity of 0.078 is that prediction as a measurement.

  (d) BUILT CROWN DIAMETER — a CROSS-ZONE CONTRACT (design derived
      TREE_SPACING_FOREST from it), so it is reported whether or not it flatters.
        DaleOak 16.5 -> 14.9 m   RiverBirch 5.7 -> 4.8   ValeWillow 10.1 -> 8.5
        GreatOak 46.3 -> 49.6
      The three ordinary species lost 10-16 % of built width: foliage grown on
      shoots sits inboard of foliage scattered through a volume. Inside the
      suite's bands, and the suite's birch floor still passes — but it is a real
      narrowing and design should see it. TWO WAYS OF HIDING IT WERE TRIED AND
      REJECTED IN THE FILES: widening `crown_width_frac` to restore the mean
      (holds the mean, pushes the widest variants through design's ceiling — the
      suite caught it) and leaning the leaf masses outward (costs the willow
      49 % of its cards to containment).

  (e) SHIMMER, measured FIRST as instructed. tools/measure_speckle.py, one 0.05 m
      stride at RUN_SPEED, DFN_WIND_FREEZE=120, 640x360, threshold 64.
      Zero control (same arm, eye still): 0.000 %, worst flip 0.0 — clean, so
      the threshold-64 readings below are real.
        near canopy   0.403 % -> 0.449 %   worst flip 243.5 -> 243.5 (unchanged)
        treeline      0.001 % -> 0.007 %   worst flip  79.8 -> 108.9
      The cost is real, small, and in the wrong direction: +0.046 points of
      screen under the crowns, and the worst single-pixel flip does not move
      there at all.
      ON THE GROUND POSE the TUFT row was taken from (DFN_FLORA_EYE=51,650,
      yaw 1.047, pitch -0.35) BOTH ARMS measure 0.001 % of frame, i.e. 32-48
      pixels, and the worst flip did not repeat across runs today: 60.5, 71.4,
      91.5 and 93.5 were all measured, on both arms. At that pixel count the
      worst flip is a ONE-PIXEL statistic. TUFT_SPECKLE_WORST_FLIP's 61.6 no
      longer describes this build in EITHER arm, so the 2.4-unit headroom cannot
      be certified either way from here and the row needs re-taking by the zone
      that owns the tuft layer. Reported rather than quietly read as "fine".

  (f) CANOPY TRANSMITTANCE, for `light`'s forest-floor dappling question: the
      open share of a crown's own plan footprint to a vertical ray.
        DaleOak 0.144 -> 0.285   RiverBirch 0.127 -> 0.194   ValeWillow 0.130 -> 0.272
      It nearly DOUBLED, which was not the expected direction: horizontal
      optical depth rose while vertical transmittance rose too, because masses
      clumped on limbs leave real holes in plan where an evenly-filled envelope
      leaves none. Note the number treats every card as a SOLID quad and so
      UNDERSTATES what gets through.

WHAT THESE FRAMES DO NOT CERTIFY

  - THE SUITE IS 47/49, NOT GREEN, and both failures are named rather than
    worked around:
      * "the canopy presents 229 m^2/tree" — its half-density control now passes
        the floor (229.9 against 229). That is the sub-check design §10.15.2
        already RETIRED as a gate, in writing, with "do not re-baseline it";
        the crown being fuller is exactly the condition it described.
      * "the plane-tilt DISTRIBUTION" — 6 clusters of ~1700. The suite groups
        cards into clusters by a 0.05 m proximity key, and envelope containment
        can slide two different clusters onto one point, which then reads as one
        cluster of six cards with two flat ones. A geometry artefact of
        containment, made likelier by a fuller crown; 0.35 % of clusters.
  - A FOURTH DISAGREEMENT ABOUT ONE QUANTITY, found and worked around rather
    than fixed, because the fix is in a file this zone does not own: the scrap
    floor is one function now, but its ARGUMENT is computed two ways. The
    emitter uses this INSTANCE's crown radius (after allometry, the width draw
    and the maturity tier); the suite can only recover the species nominal from
    a finished mesh. At the sapling tier those differ by 1/0.4^0.35 = 1.39. The
    emitter now applies the floor against the LARGER of the two, which can only
    drop more scraps — but the right end state is one definition both sides CALL.
  - THE GREAT OAK IS STILL DENSER THAN A FOREST OAK: 19.96 layers against 6.27.
    Direction fixed (from 81), destination not reached. The lever is the mass
    SIZE, which is free, not the triangle budget — see the note to lead.
  - The great oak's crown is HALF the width its own rule asks for: built 49.6 m
    diameter, i.e. a 24.8 m radius on a 41 m tree, against «нижняя часть кроны в
    радиусе равна высоте». That shortfall is older than this change (HEAD: 46.3)
    and it is not addressed here.
  - Placement, spacing and rarity are core's and design's; every great oak in
    these frames is DFN_FLORA_GREAT_OAK, a capture-only promotion.

WHY IT WAS TAKEN: this zone's brief carried a defect nobody had a number for —
"at the far frame the giant draws as bare wood while the ordinary oaks around it
carry foliage". It was worth shooting because it is the giant's own read
distance (Rule 33 puts an 80 m crown at 2.4 km) and because the LOD ladder
measured suspicious: at Silhouette the great oak emits 66 wood triangles and
ZERO cards, i.e. one green ellipsoid 50 m wide, which is precisely the "green
hill instead of an oak" that GIANT_OAKS §4 predicts.

WHAT IT ACTUALLY SHOWS, AND IT IS NOT WHAT THE BRIEF SAID: the giants are not
bare. THE WHOLE CANOPY IS THREE TIMES TOO BRIGHT AT THIS RANGE, and everything
in the band — giants and ordinary oaks alike — is pale. Measured on these
frames, the greenest tenth of each band (the part that is unambiguously
foliage rather than sky between trunks):

    near canopy, 5-30 m     rgb  39  54  32     (the shipped foliage value)
    treeline, 70-200 m      rgb  48  63  41
    distant canopy, 400 m   rgb 115 135 121     <- 2.5x the value, same hue

AND THE HUE SURVIVES: green still leads blue by +14 at 400 m against +22 near.
That distinction is the diagnosis, and it eliminates the remaining candidate's
first half. Haze LERPs toward the SKY colour, and the sky in this frame measures
rgb 153 165 181 — a canopy pulled toward it would gain BLUE fastest. This one
keeps its hue and multiplies its VALUE. So the distant canopy is not being
hazed and it is not being whitened by the atlas (already eliminated in
FloraCards.cpp: texels outside the outline stay at rgb 0). It is being LIT
differently, multiplicatively, at range — render's, and now with a number on it.

IDENTICAL IN BOTH ARMS (115/135/121 against 114/135/120), so it is neither
caused nor cured by leaves growing from branches. Recorded here because these
are the frames that carry it, not because it is this zone's to fix.

STILL NOT ANSWERED BY THIS FRAME: whether a great oak reads as a landmark at its
own distance. It cannot be answered while every crown in the band is at 2.5x its
value — the giants are as pale as their neighbours, so the comparison the frame
was taken to make is not available in it. Re-take after the value defect.
