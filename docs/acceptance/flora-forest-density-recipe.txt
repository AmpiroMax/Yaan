THE QUESTION

  The user: «леса должны быть плотнее, расстояние между деревьями нужно
  уменьшить в 2-3 раза». 15 / 2.5 = 6 m, so TREE_SPACING_FOREST 12-18 -> 5-7
  (the placement lattice reads the band's MEAN).

  That turns core's §5.12 guard red — the share of the massif's LOW silhouette
  hidden by trees from 350 m west goes 0.317 -> 0.580 against a < 0.50 ceiling.
  Measured across the band, one variable:

      band (mean)     trees    §5.12 hidden fraction
      12-18 (15 m)     2144        0.317   <- the guard was written here
       9-11 (10 m)     4723        0.495
       7-9  (8 m)      7417        0.530
       5-7  (6 m)     13446        0.580   <- red

  The guard is a SURROGATE for "the massif reads as a massif and not as
  something growing out of the forest". It was written when the forest stood
  three times thinner and its 0.50 was never derived against today's request —
  so the guard cannot be lowered to fit us, and it cannot be the verdict either.
  The frame is the verdict (Rule 27).

THE VANTAGE IS §5.12'S OWN

  §5.12 judges from crag.center - {350, 0} = (480, 200) at PLAYER_EYE_HEIGHT,
  looking at the crag (830, 200). Tour's massif_verdict step is exactly that
  aim, so the shoot is the guard's own standpoint rather than a flattering one.

  DFN_MASSIF_PROBE=1 DFN_MASSIF_EYE=480,200 DFN_TIME=0.30 \
  DFN_TOUR=1 DFN_TOUR_DIR=<dir> DFN_WIND_FREEZE=120 DFN_NULL_PHYSICS=1 \
  DFN_INTERNAL_RES=640x360 DFN_PALETTE=0 DFN_CLOUD=0 DFN_MENU=0 \
  ./build_flora/engine/app/dfn_app | tee <dir>/run.log
  then: python3 tools/archive_frame.py <src> <dst> 640

  DFN_TIME=0.30 is design's own choice for this frame and it is the right
  instrument here: a landmark reads by VALUE AGAINST SKY (§1.5), so a backlit
  dark mass is the purest form of the question.

WHY THIS PAIR IS NOT ONE BINARY, AND WHAT WAS DONE INSTEAD

  TREE_SPACING_FOREST is a generated compile-time constant read by
  WorldgenScatter, which is not this zone's file, so there is no dose door to
  open and the arms are two builds. The danger that rule guards against is a
  tree that six agents are committing to: a rebuild picks up everything else
  that landed in between, and the pair then answers "did anything change".

  So it was verified rather than hoped. Immediately before each build:
    git rev-parse HEAD                     -> 9d79c6b, identical
    shasum over every .cpp/.h/.json in engine/ and games/, sorted, hashed
                                           -> 3f7f9c68..., identical
  The only difference between the two binaries is the two NUMBERS rows. Both
  builds ran inside four minutes of each other. State this whenever the pair is
  quoted; do not quietly present it as a single-binary pair.

THE ANSWER: THE MOUNTAIN STILL READS

  Measured on the archived frames, over the massif's own 186 columns (x 285-470
  at 640x360), classifying each column's topmost non-sky pixel as rock, wood or
  leaf:

      skyline owned by the MOUNTAIN     15 m: 154 of 186 (83 %)
                                         6 m: 153 of 186 (82 %)
      columns the trees took over        11
      crest line pixel-IDENTICAL         91 of the mountain's 154 columns

  The peak, the ridge and the whole right flank stand against the sky unchanged.
  What the denser forest eats is the TOE — the low silhouette below the
  cliffline, which is precisely the quantity §5.12 measures and precisely what a
  closed forest in front of a mountain does. The guard is not broken; it is
  measuring the thing it says it measures, and that thing is now allowed to be
  bigger. Re-deriving it is design's, not ours to lower.

THE SECOND FINDING, WHICH IS LARGER THAN THE ONE THE FRAMES WERE SHOT FOR

  In the forest band of the same frames (x 0-280, y 95-200, i.e. the treeline at
  roughly 70-250 m):

                        share of band that is SKY   share that is DARK WOOD
      15 m                        66 %                       25 %
       6 m                        23 %                       75 %

  At 6 m the treeline is three quarters TRUNK. The forest does not read as a
  canopy that has closed up; it reads as a palisade of bare dark poles carrying
  pale specks. Denser spacing did not create this — it multiplied it.

  THE CAUSE IS IN THE GEOMETRY, NOT IN THE PLACEMENT, and it is measured
  (triangles per tree, mean of 12 variants, wood stream vs card stream):

      species  LOD        wood   cards   cards' share
      oak      Full        802     198       0.20
      oak      Reduced     396     123       0.24
      pine     Full        619     179       0.22
      pine     Reduced     442     102       0.19
      birch    Full        926     185       0.17
      birch    Reduced     409      98       0.19
      willow   Full        837     174       0.17
      willow   Reduced     397      99       0.20

  A tree spends FOUR FIFTHS of its budget on wood at every distance, and the
  LOD ladder halves both streams together — it never shifts the BALANCE. That is
  the wrong shape for a level of detail: at 200 m a twig is sub-pixel and the
  crown is the whole object, so a distant tree should shed WOOD and keep
  FOLIAGE. Ours does the opposite by construction, and the frame is what that
  looks like.

WHAT THESE FRAMES DO NOT CERTIFY

  - They do not certify the near canopy: at this vantage every tree is at
    Reduced LOD. The near frames are flora-leaves-from-branches-near-*.
  - They do not certify §5.12's ceiling. They answer whether the mountain reads;
    whether 0.50 is still the right number is design's to re-derive.
  - The pale, desaturated look of the foliage at this range is the canopy VALUE
    defect already recorded in flora-leaves-from-branches-recipe.txt (2.5x too
    bright at 400 m, hue surviving, identical in both flora arms). It is
    render's, it is not caused by spacing, and it is visible in both frames.
