THE COLONNADE, RUN TO GROUND (13.08.2026) — flora-colonnade-*-3353b17.png

The frames above showed the treeline reading as a palisade of dark poles. Four
explanations were tested. THREE WERE REFUTED BY MEASUREMENT and are written down
in full, because the refutations cost more than the answer and the next agent
will otherwise have them again.

THE INSTRUMENT FIRST, BECAUSE THE FIRST VERSION OF IT WAS WRONG AND THAT MATTERS

  The first reading — "the band is 75 % dark wood" — classified pixels by
  BRIGHTNESS (channel sum < 210). The shipped near-canopy foliage measures
  rgb 39/54/32, i.e. a sum of 125. IT WAS COUNTING DARK FOLIAGE AS WOOD. Rule 41
  inside my own instrument, and it was reported to LEAD before it was caught.

  The sound instrument is the MATERIAL door, which the engine already had:
  DFN_FLORA_ONLY=1 draws wood only, =2 draws cards only. Shoot all three (both
  materials and the composite) from one binary at one pose, then a pixel belongs
  to whichever material frame the composite MATCHES — that is who stands in
  front, which is the thing the eye is arguing about. All three are archived.

  Recipe: the massif-verdict command above, plus DFN_FLORA_ONLY=1 and =2.

  today, whole treeline band:  sky 23.7 %  WOOD in front 58.6 %
                               CARD in front 4.8 %  ambiguous 12.9 %
  ...and per row, wood outweighs foliage at EVERY height of the frame: 35 %
  against 15 % in the middle of the canopy mass, 60-100 % below it.

REFUTED 1 — "the LOD ladder halves both streams together"

  True of the tree and irrelevant to the frame. Forcing the whole scatter to the
  far LOD (DFN_FLORA_FORCE_LOD, ScatterBatcher) moves the band by nothing:
      wood coverage  Full 72.1 %  far 73.1 %      card coverage  41.0 % / 41.0 %
  Cutting 60 % of every tree's branch segments does not thin the stand by a
  percentage point.

REFUTED 2 — "so the fix is the far LOD; wire the ladder"

  Same measurement, and it is the reason no selection machinery was written. The
  far LOD is still worth wiring for COST (579 triangles against 1000, on 13446
  trees), and the switch distance is derived and handed over: the finest wood
  the generator emits is SHADOW_MIN_DIAMETER = 0.35 m, which subtends one pixel
  at 0.35 x 234.6 = 82 m, so a swap there cannot pop BY CONSTRUCTION. What stops
  it being a one-line change is that scatter batches are baked once per chunk at
  ChunkLoaded (App.cpp) and the batcher never sees the camera.

REFUTED 3 — "the edge effect: our edge trees have 8-11 m of bare bole"

  A real forest reads as canopy from outside because its edge trees carry
  foliage nearly to the ground. Ours are built the same at the edge as in the
  middle. Measured with DFN_FLORA_CROWNBASE, it goes the WRONG WAY:
      crown base 0.45-0.53 (today)  sky 23.7 %  wood 58.6 %  card 4.8 %
      crown base 0.30               sky 26.1 %  wood 57.1 %  card 3.4 %
      crown base 0.18               sky 28.7 %  wood 55.5 %  card 2.7 %
  The foliage budget is a fixed cluster count, so a lower base spreads the same
  leaf over a taller crown and thins it.

WHAT IS ACTUALLY TRUE, AND IT IS ARITHMETIC

  Trunk coverage of a view through a stand of depth D at spacing s with boles d
  across is 1 - (1 - d/s)^(D/s). At d = 0.6 m:

      spacing   250 m of forest      120 m       60 m
      15 m           49 %
      10 m           79 %
       8 m           91 %
       6 m           99 %              88 %       65 %

  At 6 m the BOLES ALONE tile the view within about forty rows, whatever the
  crowns do. The colonnade is a direct consequence of the spacing the user
  asked for, not a defect inside the tree, and no rearrangement of a tree's own
  budget can touch it.

  Note the 15 m row: the colonnade was already there at half strength and nobody
  named it. The new spacing did not create it, it completed it.

  Three levers exist and only one of them takes nothing away from the user:
  the spacing (his request), the bole diameter, or THE DEPTH OF THE VIEW. In a
  real forest forty rows are not visible because the air and the near crowns eat
  the sight line within tens of metres; ours is nearly clear at 250 m. That is
  render/light's air, or a design rule about view depth in woodland — and at 6 m
  spacing it only has to bring the visible depth down to about 60 m for the
  trunks to fall from 99 % to 65 %.

ONE SENTENCE WORTH KEEPING, FROM THE FAR-LOD WORK RATHER THAN FROM THESE FRAMES

  A DISTANT TREE ONE SIZE SMALLER IS NOT A LEVEL OF DETAIL, IT IS A DIFFERENT
  TREE. Optical depth is card area over crown silhouette, so it can always be
  "improved" by shrinking the crown; any far-LOD criterion written on it needs
  the built width asserted beside it or it will be met by making the tree small.

THE AIR (13.08.2026) — THE THIRD LEVER, MEASURED AND REFUTED
================================================================================

FIRST, THE CLOCKS. Every reading in this file was re-taken with

    DFN_VISTIME=100 DFN_WIND_FREEZE=100

after the sky was found to run on two clocks (sun and moon from the frame
number, cloud drift and the wind envelope from the wall clock), which put 1.79 %
of pixels in play — all of it in the sky and the treetops, i.e. exactly the band
these measurements read. With the clocks pinned, two runs of one recipe are
BYTE-IDENTICAL (verified here: same sha over the archived frame). Pin them in
any flora recipe that has sky in it.

The conclusions did not move. The readings did, by a point or two, and these are
the pinned ones:

    treeline band, Full (shipped)   wood coverage 74.4 %   card coverage 42.1 %
    treeline band, forced far LOD   wood coverage 75.5 %   card coverage 42.1 %
    who stands in front, shipped    sky 21.2 %  WOOD 60.9 %  CARD 4.9 %

THE HYPOTHESIS. In a real forest forty rows are not visible, because the air and
the near crowns eat the sight line within tens of metres. Ours is nearly clear at
250 m. So: give the woodland its share of air and the colonnade becomes a mass.

REFUTED, AND FOR A REASON THAT SHOULD HAVE BEEN OBVIOUS BEFOREHAND — WHICH IS
WHY IT WAS MEASURED. Sweeping the haze scale length (DFN_HAZE), one variable,
clocks pinned, material door for the ownership:

    haze L        sky    WOOD in front   CARD in front   wood:card
    airless      20.2 %      61.0 %          5.1 %         11.9
    600 m today  21.2 %      60.9 %          4.9 %         12.4
    300 m        23.1 %      59.9 %          4.5 %         13.4
    150 m        42.3 %      43.4 %          3.4 %         12.7
    80 m         95.1 %       3.0 %          0.7 %          4.5

THE RATIO DOES NOT MOVE. Haze is a per-pixel blend toward the sky colour by
DEPTH; it cannot change WHICH SURFACE IS IN FRONT. The wood that owns these
pixels is the NEAR trunk rows, and a near surface is exactly what depth-fog
cannot remove. Past 150 m of scale length the forest stops being wood by
dissolving into the sky, which is not the same thing as reading as canopy.

AND IT COSTS THE LANDMARK FAST. The massif's own 186 columns, mean luminance
step across the crest, against the airless control:

    haze L        crest contrast    retention
    airless             84.4          1.000
    600 m (today)       50.5          0.598
    300 m               34.9          0.414
    150 m               33.6          0.398
    80 m                25.5          0.302

Design's HAZE_SILHOUETTE_RETENTION_MIN is 0.667. (Stated with its caveat: that
constant was written for design's own verdict vantage, and this is §5.12's at
350 m, so the FLOOR may not transfer — the DIRECTION does, and it is steep.)
The air fades the mountain faster than it merges the trunks.

AND THE AIR CANNOT GREEN §5.12 IN ANY CASE. That guard is a geometric ray trace
over tree positions and heights in the generator; it never renders a pixel. No
change to haze, or to any other render term, can move it by a thousandth.

SO THERE IS NO LEVER INSIDE FLORA AT THIS SPACING. Ownership is decided by the
nearest surface, and within the first 60 m alone the boles cover 65 % of the
view at 6 m spacing. Bringing that to 30 % would need a bole of 0.21 m — a
sapling. The three levers named earlier reduce to one: d/s, the bole over the
spacing. The picture IS the spacing, and what to do about that is the user's
call, not ours.
